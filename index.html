The Inconvenient Foundation

What is context engineering, and why didn't your vendor mention it?

"Context engineering is the discipline of designing what your AI sees. It is, almost certainly, why your last deployment underperformed."

When you interact with an AI, you see a chat window. Behind every response, the model receives a carefully assembled package: instructions about how to behave, documents pulled from a database, results from tools and APIs, and the prior conversation. The model doesn't know anything else.

The quality of that package — what's included, what's left out, how it's structured — determines whether the AI is useful. Vendors demonstrate this with curated demos. Production deployments are less curated. That gap is a context engineering problem.

This is distinct from prompt engineering, which is writing one better question. Context engineering is a systems discipline: how do you design the entire information environment that shapes AI behavior across thousands of interactions, most of which you will never personally review?

Why this is now your problem

  • Sets the quality ceiling of any AI deployment

What You're Actually Paying For

The four things you're probably not managing

Every AI interaction — from your enterprise chatbot to your "AI-powered" product feature — is assembled from the same four layers. Your vendor managed them for the demo. Now it's your turn.

LAYER 01 — INSTRUCTIONS

System Prompt

Standing instructions that tell the model who it is, what it can and cannot do, and how to behave. Set once, applied to every interaction.

"You are a procurement assistant. Always cite contract clauses. Escalate requests over $50k. Never reveal supplier pricing."

LAYER 02 — KNOWLEDGE

Retrieved Documents

Relevant content fetched from your organization's knowledge base — policies, manuals, records — injected at query time so the AI reasons over real data.

→ Fetched: Q3 supplier contract, updated safety policy, last 6 months of incident reports

LAYER 03 — ACTIONS

Tool Results

Output from APIs and databases the AI can call — live inventory, calendar availability, CRM data — fed back into context so the model can act, not just advise.

→ inventory_check("SKU-4421") → {"stock": 230, "reorder_point": 100}

LAYER 04 — HISTORY

Conversation History

Prior turns of the conversation, giving the model memory within a session. Managing what gets kept, summarized, or dropped is a key engineering challenge.

→ Keeping: last 8 messages. Summarized: earlier scope discussion. Dropped: boilerplate.

The token budget constraint

AI models can only process a fixed amount of text at once — their "context window." Measured in tokens (roughly ¾ of a word each), modern models handle 100k–200k tokens. The engineering challenge is deciding what earns a spot in that budget. Too little context and the AI lacks information; too much and performance degrades. Getting this balance right is a design problem your team needs to own.

Playbook

How to implement in your organization

These steps apply regardless of your industry or scale. Start with one use case and expand.

1

Identify a high-value, high-frequency task

Pick a task your team does dozens of times daily that requires referencing internal knowledge — answering policy questions, drafting standard documents, classifying requests. The combination of frequency and knowledge-dependence makes it the best first candidate.

Good candidates: contract review, customer inquiry routing, compliance checking, report drafting.
2

Map the knowledge the task requires

List every document, database, or data source a skilled employee would consult to complete the task well. This is your candidate pool for retrieved context. Focus on sources that are frequently updated, authoritative, and currently hard to search quickly.

3

Write the system prompt

Define the AI's role, scope, tone, escalation rules, and hard constraints. Treat it like a precise job description: specific about what to do, explicit about what not to do, clear about when to hand off to a human.

The best system prompts are written collaboratively by the domain expert and the AI builder — neither alone produces the best result.
4

Build a retrieval pipeline

Connect your knowledge sources so relevant content can be fetched at query time. Start simple: index your most important documents, run semantic search, inject the top results. Measure retrieval quality early — garbage in, garbage out.

5

Evaluate and iterate

Build a test set of 50–100 real examples with known correct answers. Poor performance almost always traces back to a specific context problem: wrong documents retrieved, system prompt gaps, or context that's too long or too sparse.

Build evaluation before you scale. It's much harder to fix quality problems after users have formed habits around a broken system.
6

Govern and maintain

Context engineering is ongoing, not a one-time project. Documents go stale, policies change, new edge cases emerge. Assign ownership of the system prompt and knowledge sources. Treat it like a living system, not a shipped product.

Application

Context engineering across industries

The same four-layer architecture applies everywhere. What changes is the knowledge, the constraints, and the workflow.

Client advisor assistant

A wealth management firm deploys an AI assistant for advisors preparing client meetings. The system retrieves the client's portfolio, recent transactions, risk profile, and relevant research — assembled fresh before each meeting.

Context injected

Client portfolio summary · Q3 performance report · Risk tolerance profile · Pending tax events · 3 relevant research notes

Loan underwriting support

Underwriters use AI to surface relevant precedents when assessing unusual applications. The system retrieves similar past decisions, current lending policy, and regulatory guidelines specific to the applicant's situation.

Context injected

Lending policy v4.2 · 5 comparable past decisions · Applicant financial summary · Current rate schedule · Regulatory flags

Clinical documentation assistant

Clinicians use AI to draft discharge summaries. The system retrieves the patient's current visit notes, relevant history, active medications, and allergy list — with strict constraints and mandatory human review before any use.

Context injected

Visit notes (today) · Relevant past diagnoses · Active medications · Known allergies · Discharge template

Benefits navigation assistant

A health insurer deploys a benefits assistant that retrieves the specific member's plan details, coverage tiers, and claim history to answer eligibility questions accurately — connected to live plan data, not static FAQs.

Context injected

Member plan document · Current deductible status · Prior authorization history · Network provider list

Contract review assistant

Associates use AI to flag non-standard clauses. The system retrieves the firm's standard playbook for that contract type and any client-specific deviations on file, enabling systematic comparison across hundreds of pages.

Context injected

Firm playbook (contract type) · Client fallback positions · Contract under review · Prior negotiation history

Regulatory compliance monitor

A compliance team monitors regulatory changes. The AI retrieves the latest guidance relevant to the firm's practice areas and maps it against current internal policies to surface gaps requiring attention.

Context injected

Regulatory updates (last 30 days) · Firm's current policy documents · Jurisdiction mapping · Prior gap analyses

Maintenance troubleshooting

Technicians in the field use an AI assistant that retrieves the equipment's service history, the relevant manual section, and similar past fault cases when diagnosing failures — without needing an expert on site.

Context injected

Equipment service history · Manual section (fault code) · 5 similar past incidents · Parts availability

Procurement assistant

Buyers use AI to draft purchase orders and evaluate supplier bids. The system retrieves active contracts, the approved supplier list, budget availability, and procurement policy to ensure every order complies automatically.

Context injected

Approved supplier list · Active contracts · Budget availability (live) · Procurement policy · Item specs

Personalized tutoring

An adaptive learning platform retrieves each student's prior performance, current module, and identified misconceptions to tailor explanations and difficulty — giving every student a different experience from the same model.

Context injected

Student performance history · Current module content · Identified misconceptions · Preferred explanation style

Admissions review support

Admissions teams use AI to draft evaluation summaries. The system retrieves the program's rubric, the institution's stated values, and comparable past decisions to produce consistent structured assessments.

Context injected

Program rubric and criteria · Application materials · 3 comparable past decisions · Program constraints

Test your understanding

Context engineering quiz

Seven questions designed to test real understanding, not recall.

Q 01 / 07

Which best describes what "context engineering" is?

Correct. Context engineering designs the full information package the model receives — instructions, knowledge, tools, and history. Distinct from prompt engineering (one better question) or model training (modifying the model itself).
Q 02 / 07

Your company wants an AI assistant that always follows your procurement policy, which updates quarterly. What context layer is most important to get right?

Correct. Policies that change regularly should live in a retrieval system, not the system prompt. Fetching the current policy version at query time ensures the AI always reasons from the latest source. Hardcoding it in the system prompt (D) will drift out of date every quarter.
Q 03 / 07

What does RAG stand for, and what problem does it solve?

Correct. RAG is the dominant technique for connecting AI to organizational knowledge. Rather than retraining the model, you build a search index over your documents and inject the most relevant content into context at query time. The AI then reasons over real, current data — not just what it learned during training.
Q 04 / 07

An AI assistant at a law firm gives inconsistent answers about client confidentiality — sometimes advising caution, sometimes not mentioning it at all. What is the most likely cause?

Correct. Inconsistent behavior on a critical rule almost always means the rule isn't clearly stated in the system prompt. If confidentiality guidance must appear in every applicable response, it needs to be an explicit requirement — not left for the model to infer. Gaps in the system prompt produce gaps in behavior.
Q 05 / 07

Why is the "context window" an important constraint to understand when building AI applications?

Correct. The context window is measured in tokens and is finite. All four context layers must fit within it. This creates real tradeoffs: more retrieved documents means less conversation history, longer instructions means less room for data. Understanding your token budget and spending it well is a core engineering task.
Q 06 / 07

You're writing a system prompt for a customer service AI assistant. Which approach is more appropriate?

Correct. Option A is the kind of generic instruction that produces inconsistent, uncontrolled behavior. A good system prompt is specific about scope, explicit about constraints, and clear about escalation conditions. Option B names the domain, sets hard rules, and defines a handoff point — a behavioral policy document, not a vague aspiration.
Q 07 / 07

After deploying an AI assistant that performed well in testing, a manager notices quality has degraded six months later. What is the most likely explanation?

Correct. Context engineering is ongoing maintenance. Policies change, products get updated, edge cases emerge. If the documents in the retrieval system aren't kept current, or the system prompt isn't revised as the business evolves, the AI gives answers that were correct at deployment but are wrong now. Assigning clear ownership of the context is as important as building it.
