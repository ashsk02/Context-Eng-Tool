Foundation

What Is Context Engineering?

You have probably heard of prompt engineering — the art of crafting better instructions for an AI. Context engineering is the bigger discipline it sits inside.

"Context engineering is the practice of designing, structuring, and optimizing all the information you provide to an AI system — not just your instructions — to reliably produce accurate, useful outputs."

When you interact with an AI model, the model only knows what is currently in its context window — its working memory for that interaction. Context engineering is the discipline of deciding exactly what goes into that window.

Prompt engineering focuses on the wording of your question. Context engineering asks: what documents, data, rules, history, and role definitions should surround that question? Getting that right is what separates a generic AI tool from one that acts like an expert in your domain.

💡

Analogy: Imagine briefing a new contractor. You would not just ask your question cold — you would hand them the site plan, safety procedures, relevant regulations, and background on the project. Context engineering is that briefing, done systematically for AI.

🎯

More Than Prompts

Prompt engineering tweaks the question. Context engineering designs the entire information environment — documents, rules, examples, data — the AI reasons from.

🏭

Domain-Specific Power

AI models are generalists. Context engineering makes them specialists — by injecting your procedures, standards, and institutional knowledge at query time.

🔒

Control & Reliability

Well-engineered context produces predictable, auditable behavior. The AI reasons from what you gave it, not from vague training assumptions.

Mechanics

How the Context Window Works

Every AI model has a context window — the total text it can read and reason over in one interaction. Think of it as the model's short-term memory: everything it can "see" right now.

Modern models can hold hundreds of thousands of words in their context window. The question is not can you fit your information in — it is which information to include, and how to structure it, so the model reasons correctly.

📚

RAG — Retrieval Augmented Generation

Rather than loading your entire document library into every query, RAG automatically retrieves only the most relevant sections and injects them into the context window. This is how AI can "know" your specific procedures without being retrained.

🧠

Why Context Quality Matters

An AI with poor context gives confident but generic answers. An AI with well-engineered context gives answers grounded in your actual data, standards, and procedures — dramatically reducing the risk of hallucination or irrelevant output.

Best Practices

Five Principles of Effective Context

These principles apply whether you are engineering a one-off query or designing an enterprise AI system.

1

Relevance

Only include information that is directly useful for the task. Irrelevant content dilutes the model's focus and can introduce noise.

2

Precision

Be specific about the AI's role, the format you want, and the constraints it must respect. Vague context produces vague outputs.

3

Structure

Organize context clearly — labeled sections, consistent formatting, hierarchy. The model reasons better from well-structured inputs.

4

Completeness

Include everything the model needs to answer correctly without hallucinating. If a standard or regulation is critical, put it in the context.

5

Freshness

Context should reflect current facts. Stale procedures, outdated well data, or superseded regulations lead to incorrect outputs.

Industry Application

Oil & Gas Use Cases

The O&G sector generates enormous volumes of domain-specific data — well reports, geological surveys, HSE procedures, regulatory filings. Context engineering is the bridge between that institutional knowledge and useful AI outputs.

Well Planning & Risk Analysis

An AI assistant that helps engineers identify drilling risks and anomalies by reasoning over project-specific data — not just general knowledge.

Context you would provide:
  • Well plan and proposed trajectory
  • Offset well data and formation tops
  • Geological summary and pore-pressure profile
  • Company-specific drilling procedures
  • Previous NPT incident reports for similar wells

What this enables

  • Risk flags grounded in your actual well history — not generic best practices
  • Comparison against offset wells in your own database
  • Procedure compliance checking against your specific standards
  • Faster pre-drill review with auditable reasoning

Incident Investigation & Learning

AI that can analyze incident reports against your safety management system to identify root cause patterns and suggest preventive actions.

Context you would provide:
  • The incident report and witness statements
  • Relevant sections of your HSE management system
  • Historical incident database (similar events)
  • Applicable regulatory requirements
  • Bow-tie or fault-tree framework templates

What this enables

  • Root cause analysis grounded in your SMS, not generic frameworks
  • Pattern detection across historical incidents
  • Draft investigation reports with traceable sources
  • Action items mapped to specific procedures

Predictive Maintenance & Troubleshooting

AI-powered maintenance support that reasons over real sensor data, equipment history, and manufacturer documentation to diagnose issues faster.

Context you would provide:
  • Current sensor readings and alarm logs
  • Equipment maintenance history
  • OEM manuals and troubleshooting guides
  • Spare parts availability from CMMS
  • Previous work orders for this asset

What this enables

  • Fault diagnosis that accounts for this specific asset's history
  • Recommended actions with references to the actual manual
  • Reduced diagnostic time for field technicians
  • Knowledge transfer from experienced engineers to newer staff

Regulatory Compliance Checking

AI that checks your documents, procedures, or permit applications against the specific regulations that apply to your operation.

Context you would provide:
  • The document or application being reviewed
  • Applicable regulations (e.g. NOPSEMA, CAPP, API standards)
  • Your existing approved procedures
  • Previous regulator correspondence
  • Compliance checklist templates

What this enables

  • Gap analysis against the actual regulation text — not generic summaries
  • Faster permit application drafting with compliance evidence
  • Consistent checks across large document volumes
  • Audit trails showing which regulation each finding maps to

Onboarding & Competency Development

AI tutors that answer new employee questions using your actual procedures and standards — not generic internet knowledge.

Context you would provide:
  • Role-specific standard operating procedures
  • Competency frameworks and learning objectives
  • Site-specific safety rules and emergency procedures
  • Process flow diagrams and equipment schematics
  • Frequently asked questions from past trainees

What this enables

  • 24/7 Q&A tutor grounded in your specific procedures
  • Consistent answers — no more "ask someone else for a second opinion"
  • Faster time to competency for new hires
  • Scalable training support across sites and shifts

Implementation

Your Implementation Roadmap

You do not need to boil the ocean. Most organizations start with one high-value use case and expand. Here is a practical path.

1
Weeks 1–2

Map Your Knowledge Assets

Before you can engineer context, you need to know what you have. Audit your key document repositories — procedures, reports, standards, manuals. Identify which are current, structured, and high-value. Messy or outdated documents will produce poor AI outputs regardless of how good your context engineering is.

2
Weeks 2–4

Pick One High-Value Use Case

Choose a task where: (a) staff spend significant time on information retrieval or synthesis, (b) the relevant documents are reasonably up-to-date, and (c) the output can be verified before acting on it. Drilling risk analysis, compliance checking, and maintenance troubleshooting are common starting points in O&G.

3
Month 2

Design Your Context Architecture

Define: what goes in the system prompt (role, rules, format), what documents get retrieved and how (RAG), what structured data gets injected (sensor readings, work orders), and what constraints the AI must respect. Write this down as a context design document — this is your engineering specification.

4
Month 2–3

Build, Test, and Validate

Stand up a prototype with a small user group. Test against known cases — feed in scenarios where you know the correct answer and verify the AI gets it right. Subject matter experts should review outputs before any workflow integration. Document failure modes: what context gaps cause wrong answers?

5
Ongoing

Govern and Iterate

Establish ownership: who maintains the context (keeps documents current, updates the system prompt, adds new procedures)? How are errors reported and fixed? Set up a regular review cycle. Context engineering is not a one-time deployment — it is an ongoing practice of keeping your AI's information environment as sharp as your team's expertise.

⚠️

Common pitfall: Teams often focus too much on the AI model and too little on the context. The model is largely fixed — the context is where you have full control. Invest in your document quality and context architecture first.
